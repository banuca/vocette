# Murmur rename: naming shortlist

Research date: 25 September 2026. Web research only. **This is not a legal trademark clearance.** Get a trademark attorney to run a proper search before filing or spending money on the brand.

## Outcome in one line

Out of about 260 names checked, 7 survive every check I could run. **Recommendation: Vocette.** It came back clean on every check, and all five domains checked (.com, .app, get…com, .io, .ai) have no registration.

## Why this was hard (context for the reader)

Local, push-to-talk dictation apps flooded the market in 2025–26. Wispr Flow clones, vibe-coded Whisper and Parakeet wrappers, and 0-star GitHub repos have taken almost every obvious speech word. That covers English, Latin, Italian, Swedish, Swahili and Japanese words, bird names, "say/talk/speak/hush + key/type/word" compounds, and the names of historical scribes. Most rejections below are real dictation or transcription products, often with the same pitch as Murmur (hold a key, speak, text appears, private and offline). A few were registered days before this search: sanelu.com ("Speech to text, privately yours") was registered on 21 Sep 2026. Expect this to keep happening, so **secure the chosen name quickly** (domains plus a CH/EU/UK/US filing in classes 9 and 42).

More evidence that "Murmur" must go: besides MurmurType on Setapp (https://setapp.com/apps/murmur), GitHub has `thatbeautifuldream/murmur`, a system-wide macOS dictation app (https://github.com/topics/dictation-app), and "Murmure" is on the awesome-voice-typing list (https://github.com/primaprashant/awesome-voice-typing).

## Ranked shortlist

| Rank | Name | Why it fits (one line) | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vocette** | Coined "little voice" (Latin *voc-* + French *-ette*; Italian *vocette* = "little voices"). It rhymes with *cassette*, the old dictation tape. Short, ownable, with a European feel. | **Low** |
| 2 | **Quietword** | From the British idiom "a quiet word" (a private, discreet talk), so privacy and speech in one plain-English name. | **Low–medium** (Bible apps share the name) |
| 3 | **Wordlark** | Word + lark, the songbird. "For a lark" means easy and fun, so words that just come out. | **Low–medium** (a small naming agency trades as Wordlark) |
| 4 | **Fairspoken** | "Fair-spoken" means well-spoken or courteous, and "fair" carries the honest, pay-once message. | **Low** (but long and a bit obscure) |
| 5 | **Mezzavoce** | Italian musical direction *mezza voce* ("half voice": softly). Quiet speech, with a craftsman feel. | **Low** on conflicts, **medium** on spelling |
| 6 | **Wordswift** | Words + swift (fast, and also the bird). Puts speed first. | **Low–medium** |
| 7 | **Softword** | Soft (quiet, gentle) + word, with a nod to "software". | **Low–medium** (searchability) |

### Recommendation: Vocette

- It is the only candidate that is clean on every check **and** has all five domains checked unregistered: vocette.com, vocette.app, getvocette.com, vocette.io, vocette.ai.
- A coined word is the strongest kind of trademark. Quietword, Softword and Wordswift are built from dictionary words, so they are weaker to defend.
- It suggests *voice* without being a generic word. It is 7 letters and 2 syllables (voh-SET), and the French and Italian flavour suits a Geneva maker selling worldwide.
- **Caveats:**
  - It needs a one-line explainer, e.g. "Vocette — your voice, typed. Privately."
  - Some people will type "Vosette" or "Vocet". Consider buying the obvious typos.
  - "-ette" can read as a diminutive.
  - Two near neighbours exist: a small Android public-speaking app called **Voxette**, and an EU mark **VOCETRA** for teaching apparatus. Neither is a registered software or dictation mark I could find.
- **Runner-up:** Quietword, if an instantly understood English name matters more than full ownability. Its .com is listed on the Afternic domain marketplace.

## Method

Every name went through a quick screen: Mac and iOS App Store, Microsoft Store, USPTO, then domain, GitHub and Hacker News checks. Survivors then got the full check below.

- **App stores:**
  - Apple iTunes Search API, US storefront, Mac and iOS.
  - Microsoft Store storefront search API (US) and winget (community and msstore sources).
  - Google Play search page (finalists only).
  - Product Hunt search page (finalists).
  - Setapp via site-restricted web search. Setapp's own search page is rendered in the browser, so fetching it returned "no results" even for "dictation"; I did not rely on it.
- **Code and community:** GitHub repository search (name, and name plus dictation / speech to text / whisper / transcription / voice). Hacker News through the Algolia search service (Show HN, stories, comments).
- **Web:** the WebSearch tool (US index) for "<name> dictation", "<name> speech to text / voice typing", "<name> app" and "<name> company".
- **Trade marks:**
  - USPTO: live and dead marks, all classes, word-mark contains and pseudo-mark searches; class 9/42 hits flagged.
  - EUIPO eSearch: EU marks and international registrations designating the EU.
  - Swissreg (Swiss IPI): Swiss marks and international registrations designating CH.
  - TMview: filtered to classes 9 and 42, live marks. Used for about the first 100 names screened, until the service began blocking this network.
- **Domains:**
  - Registry lookup service (RDAP): Verisign for .com, Google Registry for .app, rdap.org for .ai.
  - DNS lookups via dns.google for every finalist domain, including .io. The .io RDAP route proved unreliable: it reported google.io as unregistered.
  - Where a domain is registered, I fetched the page to see what is on it.

## Limits: what I could not verify

- **UK trade marks:** the UK IPO search returned 403, and TMview, which covers the UK, blocked this network after about 100 queries (from about 17:10 CEST; still blocked at 18:07). **None of the 7 finalists has been checked against UK or national EU offices** (DE, FR, IT, Benelux and others). EUIPO and Swissreg were checked.
- **WIPO Global Brand Database:** not queried directly, because it sits behind a proof-of-work captcha. International (Madrid) registrations were covered only where they designate the EU, CH or US (EUIPO, Swissreg, USPTO).
- **Similar-sounding marks:** only the listed spelling variants were checked. No full phonetic search.
- **Domain availability:** "not registered" means the registry had no record, or DNS had no delegation, at the time of lookup. Not confirmed: whether a registrar will sell the name, premium pricing, and reserved lists. For registered domains, the owner and asking price are unknown.
- **Web search:** the index is US-based. Very new products (days old) or ones not yet indexed can be missed, as the sanelu.com case shows.
- **Legal advice:** none of this is legal advice.
