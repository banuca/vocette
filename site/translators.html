Hold a shortcut, speak your translation, let go. The text appears where your cursor is.

Your clients’ texts stay with you

By default, speech is turned into text on your PC. Nothing you dictate is sent anywhere, and recordings are never written to disk.

25 languages, no switching

Languages

25 languages, with NVIDIA’s own figures.

Vocette’s on-device engine uses NVIDIA’s Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 speech model. The table shows the word error rates NVIDIA published for that model on the FLEURS test set: the share of words that came out wrong, missing or extra, not counting punctuation and capitals. Lower is better: 5% is about one word in twenty.

These are NVIDIA’s figures, not ours. NVIDIA measured its original model. Vocette runs its own int8 version of that model, and we have measured Vocette’s accuracy only in English so far.

NVIDIA notes that its training data uses European Portuguese, while most test sets use Brazilian Portuguese.

Source: NVIDIA, parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 model card on Hugging Face, table “Multilingual ASR”, FLEURS column.

Before you start

What we can’t promise yet.

Four things to know before you rely on Vocette for client work.

  • Accuracy outside English is not measured yet

    We have tested Vocette’s accuracy only in English. Try it on your own language pair, with your own kind of text, before you rely on it.

  • Trados and memoQ are being tested

    Vocette pastes with Ctrl + V into the window you are working in. We are testing that in Trados and memoQ now. Until we have, we can’t promise it works there.

  • Clean-up is English only

    Removing fillers and following “scratch that” or “new line” work in English. In other languages, Vocette removes only hesitation sounds such as “äh” and “euh”.

  • Windows only, and not signed yet

    Vocette is for Windows 10 and 11; the Mac and Linux builds are betas. The installer is not signed yet, so Windows may warn you the first time you run it.
